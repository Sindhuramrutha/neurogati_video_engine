Subtopic: Clinical vs. Consumer Neurotechnology
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Neurotechnology can also be distinguished by its intended application. Clinical neurotechnology is used for purposes such as diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, rehabilitation, and restoration of function. Clinical EEG, neuroprosthetic systems, and implanted stimulation technologies are examples. Consumer neurotechnology is generally aimed at applications such as wellness, personal monitoring, interaction, gaming, or other non-clinical uses. Wearable neural or physiological sensors may be used in these settings. It's important not to assume that a consumer device and a clinical system provide equivalent information simply because they use similar terminology. Clinical applications require appropriate evidence for reliability, safety, and effectiveness. This distinction also highlights a central challenge in neurotechnology: a signal may be measurable, but converting that measurement into a reliable and useful application requires careful validation.
