Subtopic: Reading vs. Influencing the Nervous System
Scene type: manim

Narration:
We can also think about neurotechnology in terms of direction. When information moves from the nervous system toward a device, we can think of the system as reading from the nervous system. EEG, EMG, and many brain-computer interfaces follow this general direction. In contrast, when a device delivers stimulation intended to influence nervous-system activity, information or energy is moving toward the nervous system. Technologies such as transcranial magnetic stimulation and deep brain stimulation illustrate this direction. The distinction is useful because it gives us a simple mental model: Read, then understand, then act. Or, alternatively: Measure, then decide, then stimulate. Some advanced systems combine both. They measure the state of the nervous system, analyze it, and use the result to determine an intervention. This closed-loop concept connects recording technologies with neuromodulation, and it will become important when we discuss future neurotechnology.
