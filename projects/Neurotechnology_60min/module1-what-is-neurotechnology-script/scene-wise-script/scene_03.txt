Subtopic: The Nervous System: Electrical, Chemical, and Computational
Scene type: manim

Narration:
To understand why neurotechnology works, it helps to consider the nervous system from three complementary perspectives. First, the nervous system is an electrical system. Neurons communicate through changes in membrane potential and electrical currents. These electrical signals can sometimes be measured outside the neurons using sensors or electrodes. EEG and neural electrode recordings are examples of technologies that take advantage of this electrical activity. Second, the nervous system is also a chemical system. Neurons communicate through neurotransmitters and other biochemical processes. Although many technologies measure electrical activity, the biological processes producing those signals are strongly connected to chemical communication. Third, the nervous system is a computational system. Networks of neurons continuously transform information. Sensory systems process incoming information, neural circuits integrate it, and motor systems generate commands that ultimately influence behavior. These perspectives are not separate. Electrical and chemical processes support computation in neural circuits, and neurotechnology attempts to access measurable aspects of this complex system. This is why neurotechnology requires knowledge from several fields. Neuroscience helps us understand the biological source of the signal. Engineering provides sensors and acquisition systems. Signal processing extracts useful information. And computing and machine learning can help interpret or decode it.
