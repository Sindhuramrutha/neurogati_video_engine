Subtopic: Recording, Decoding, and Modulation
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Here's a second useful way to organize neurotechnology: three broad activities — recording, decoding, and modulation. Recording means measuring activity from the nervous system or related physiological systems. EEG, EMG, MEG, and implanted neural recordings are examples. Decoding begins after recording. Here, computational methods are used to extract information from the measured signal. For example, a brain-computer interface might analyze neural activity and estimate which movement or command a person intends to make. Modulation is different. Instead of simply observing neural activity, the technology attempts to influence it. Transcranial magnetic stimulation, deep brain stimulation, and other stimulation approaches are examples. These categories can also be combined. A system may record activity, process it, make a decision, and then deliver stimulation. This is called a closed-loop approach. This idea becomes especially important later in the course, because modern neurotechnology increasingly combines sensing, computation, and intervention within the same system.

Animation description:
Three distinct panels or columns are shown. First column 'Recording': icons for EEG, EMG, MEG, and implanted electrodes appear, with waveforms being captured. Second column 'Decoding': a brain-computer interface example, with neural waveform entering a computer and a predicted command (e.g., 'move left') emerging. Third column 'Modulation': a TMS coil over a head pulsing magnetic fields, and a DBS electrode delivering pulses. Then an animation shows a combined system: recorded waveform enters a processor, which makes a decision, which triggers stimulation back into the nervous system, forming a closed loop with rotating arrows.
