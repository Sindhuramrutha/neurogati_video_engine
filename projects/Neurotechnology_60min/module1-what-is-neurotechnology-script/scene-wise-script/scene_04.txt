Subtopic: The Basic Neurotechnology Pipeline
Scene type: manim

Narration:
One of the most useful ideas for understanding this entire course is a simple pipeline. It starts with the biological source and ends with an action: Brain or nerve, then Signal, then Sensor, then Acquisition, then Processing, then Interpretation, then Action. Let's go through this step by step. The process begins with activity in the brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves, or muscles. That biological activity produces something that can be measured, such as an electrical potential or magnetic field. A sensor then captures the measurable signal. For EEG, electrodes detect voltage differences at the scalp. For MEG, sensors detect magnetic fields associated with neural activity. For EMG, electrodes detect electrical activity related to muscle activation. The acquisition system records and digitizes the signal so that it can be stored and processed by a computer. The next stage is processing. Real biological recordings are rarely clean. They can contain noise, artifacts, interference, and other unwanted components. Processing methods can therefore be used to improve signal quality and prepare the data for analysis. After processing, we extract or interpret information from the signal. Finally, that information may lead to an action — for example, a clinical decision, a movement command for a prosthetic device, or feedback to the user. This pipeline is the backbone of the course. Later modules will focus on different parts of it in greater detail.
