Narration:
Welcome to this course on neurotechnology. Let's start with a simple question: what actually is it? Neurotechnology is a broad, interdisciplinary field that sits at the intersection of neuroscience, engineering, medicine, and computing. At its core, neurotechnology involves technologies that interact with the nervous system. These technologies may be used to record neural or physiological activity, to process and interpret that activity, to influence how the nervous system functions, or to help restore functions that have been lost or impaired. Now, here's something important to understand right away: neurotechnology is not one particular machine, and it's not one particular technique. It's a collection of technologies that operate at different levels of the nervous system and serve very different purposes. For example, electroencephalography, or EEG, records electrical activity from the scalp. Magnetic resonance imaging, or MRI, can provide structural information about the brain, while functional MRI can reveal changes associated with brain activity. Electromyography, or EMG, records electrical activity associated with muscles. Brain-computer interfaces can use recorded signals to generate commands for an external device. And other technologies, such as deep brain stimulation, work in the opposite direction by delivering stimulation to neural tissue. Throughout this course, we'll connect these technologies rather than studying them as isolated topics.
