Subtopic: Connecting the Technologies in This Course
Scene type: manim

Narration:
At this point, we can place the major technologies in this course into the framework we've developed. EEG gives us a way to record electrical activity from the scalp. ECoG and implanted electrodes allow measurements closer to neural tissue. EMG provides information about muscle activity, while nerve-conduction methods examine signals traveling through peripheral nerves. Imaging technologies such as MRI and fMRI provide another way of studying the brain, giving us structural or functional information rather than simply recording the electrical signal at the scalp. Once signals are recorded or images are acquired, processing and computational methods allow us to extract useful information. That information can then support applications such as diagnosis, brain-computer interfaces, neuroprosthetics, and rehabilitation. Finally, stimulation technologies allow us to influence nervous-system activity. So the course follows a logical progression. First, we ask what neurotechnology is. Then we learn how to record and image the nervous system. Next, we learn how to turn those measurements into information. Then we examine how that information can control devices. Finally, we look at how technology can directly modify nervous-system activity and where the field is going next.
