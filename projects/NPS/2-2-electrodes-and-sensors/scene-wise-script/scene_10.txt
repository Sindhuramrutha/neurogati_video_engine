Subtopic: 2.2.8 MEG Sensors
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Magnetoencephalography uses a different sensing principle. Instead of measuring electrical voltage, MEG measures the extremely small magnetic fields generated by neuronal activity. Therefore, MEG does not use electrodes. Instead, it employs highly sensitive magnetic sensors positioned around the participant's head. Although the sensing technology differs, the purpose remains the same: detecting neural activity so that it can enter the acquisition chain.

Animation description:
A stylized head is shown from the front, surrounded by a helmet-like array of magnetic sensor coils positioned around the scalp. Magnetic field lines, drawn as curved arrows circulating around active neural regions inside the brain, pass through the sensors. Unlike the electrode scenes, no wires or electrodes touch the skin. At the bottom, a small acquisition chain diagram fades in with the MEG sensors shown as the first block, demonstrating that the purpose remains identical to an electrode despite the different technology.
