Subtopic: 2.2.4 Surface Electrodes – Definition and Usage
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Surface electrodes are electrodes placed directly on the skin without penetrating the body. Because they do not require surgery or needle insertion, they are classified as non-invasive electrodes. Surface electrodes are among the most commonly used recording electrodes in biomedical engineering. They are widely used for EEG, surface EMG, and ECG recordings because they are safe, inexpensive, and easy to apply. Before placing a surface electrode, the skin is often cleaned and a conductive gel is applied. The gel improves electrical contact between the electrode and the skin by reducing contact resistance, allowing cleaner recordings.
