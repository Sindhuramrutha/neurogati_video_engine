Subtopic: 2.2.10 Summary and Transition
Scene type: manim

Narration:
In this section, we examined the different types of electrodes and sensors used in neural signal acquisition. Although these devices differ in their design and recording location, they all perform the same fundamental function — detecting physiological activity and transferring it to the acquisition system. Selecting an appropriate sensor always involves balancing signal quality, spatial resolution, patient safety, and invasiveness. Now that we know how biological signals are detected, another challenge immediately becomes apparent. The detected signals are extremely small — often only a few microvolts. Such weak signals cannot be processed directly. In the next section, we will explore how these signals are strengthened and prepared for further processing through amplification and signal conditioning.
