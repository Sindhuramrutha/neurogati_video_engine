Subtopic: 2.2.3 What is an Electrode?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
An electrode is a device that detects the electrical activity generated by biological tissues and transfers that signal to the recording system. An electrode does not generate electrical signals. Instead, it acts as an interface between the body and the recording instrument, allowing naturally occurring physiological signals to be measured. Think about using a thermometer to measure body temperature. The thermometer does not produce heat. It simply measures the heat already present in the body. An electrode works in a very similar way. It does not create electrical activity. Instead, it detects the electrical potentials already produced by neurons, muscles, or other biological tissues, and delivers these signals to the acquisition system for further processing. For accurate recordings, the electrode must maintain good electrical contact with the tissue. Poor contact can introduce unwanted noise, reduce signal quality, and make subsequent analysis less reliable.
