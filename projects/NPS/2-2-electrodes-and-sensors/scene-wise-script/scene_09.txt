Subtopic: 2.2.7 ECoG Grids
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Unlike EEG, Electrocorticography records electrical activity directly from the surface of the cerebral cortex. Instead of individual scalp electrodes, ECoG uses flexible electrode grids or strips that are surgically placed on the exposed cortex. Because the electrodes are much closer to the neural source, ECoG recordings provide higher signal amplitude, better spatial resolution, and an improved signal-to-noise ratio compared with EEG. However, these advantages come at the cost of requiring neurosurgery, making ECoG an invasive recording technique.
