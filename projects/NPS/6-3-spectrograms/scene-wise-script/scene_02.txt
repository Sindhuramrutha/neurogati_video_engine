Subtopic: Understanding a Spectrogram: Three Components
Scene type: manim

Narration:
A spectrogram has three main components. The first is the time axis. The horizontal axis represents time, and as we move from left to right, we observe how the signal evolves throughout the recording. The second component is the frequency axis. The vertical axis represents frequency, usually measured in Hertz. Lower frequencies appear near the bottom of the spectrogram, while higher frequencies appear near the top. And the third component is colour intensity. The colour represents the strength or energy of each frequency component at a particular time. Brighter or warmer colours generally indicate stronger activity, while darker or cooler colours indicate weaker activity.

Regions of the spectrogram light up in warm colours when discussing stronger activity and dim to cool colours when discussing weaker activity.
