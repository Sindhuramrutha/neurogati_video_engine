Subtopic: 2.4.3 Analog and Digital Signals
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Let's start with the two types of signals. An analogue signal is one whose amplitude changes continuously over time. A digital signal represents information as a sequence of discrete numerical values. The electrical signals generated by neurons are naturally analogue. As neural activity changes, the voltage changes continuously. And although this continuous representation accurately reflects physiological activity, computers can't directly interpret continuously varying voltages — digital systems store and process information as numbers. So before a neural signal can be analysed with software, it must first be converted from its analogue form into a digital representation. And that conversion is performed by the analog-to-digital converter.
