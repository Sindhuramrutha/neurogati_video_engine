Subtopic: 2.4.4 What is Sampling?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
So what exactly is sampling? Sampling is the process of measuring the amplitude of an analogue signal at regular intervals of time. Each measurement is called a sample. Think of it like taking photographs of a moving object every second. Instead of recording every instant continuously, you capture a series of individual images. Sampling works in a similar way. Rather than recording every continuous change in voltage, the acquisition system measures the signal at equally spaced time intervals. Each measurement represents the signal at that particular instant. And when thousands of samples are collected every second, they provide an accurate digital representation of the original analogue waveform. For example, suppose an EEG recording system samples the signal one thousand times every second. That means every second, the system records one thousand individual voltage measurements. Collectively, those measurements represent the original EEG waveform in digital form.
