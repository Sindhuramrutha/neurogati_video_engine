Subtopic: 2.4.6 The Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC)
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Now, the component that actually does this conversion. An analog-to-digital converter — an ADC — is an electronic component that converts an analogue electrical signal into a sequence of digital numbers. The ADC serves as the bridge between the analogue world and the digital world. After the biological signal has been amplified and conditioned, it enters the ADC. The ADC performs two essential tasks. First, it samples the analogue signal at regular time intervals. Second, it converts each sample into a numerical value that can be processed by a computer. The resulting sequence of numbers forms the digital representation of the original neural signal. Without an ADC, modern digital storage, visualisation, and signal processing simply would not be possible.
