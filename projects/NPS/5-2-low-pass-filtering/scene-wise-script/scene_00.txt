Subtopic: 5.2.1 Introduction
Scene type: manim

Narration:
In the previous section, we saw that filtering improves the quality of neural recordings by reducing unwanted signal components. But not all unwanted signals live in the same place. Some occur at very high frequencies, while others occur at much lower frequencies. To remove unwanted high-frequency components while preserving slower physiological activity, one of the most commonly used tools is the low-pass filter. In this section, we'll understand how a low-pass filter operates, how it modifies a signal, and where it's applied in neural signal processing.
