Subtopic: 5.2.4 The Concept of Cutoff Frequency
Scene type: manim

Narration:
A low-pass filter does not simply separate frequencies into two completely distinct groups. Instead, it is designed around a key parameter called the cutoff frequency. The cutoff frequency is the frequency above which signal components begin to be attenuated by the filter. Suppose a low-pass filter has a cutoff frequency of forty hertz. That means frequency components below forty hertz are allowed to pass with minimal attenuation. As the frequency increases beyond forty hertz, the filter gradually reduces the amplitude of those higher-frequency components. Crucially, the cutoff frequency is not an abrupt boundary where signals are suddenly removed. Instead, attenuation increases progressively beyond the cutoff.
