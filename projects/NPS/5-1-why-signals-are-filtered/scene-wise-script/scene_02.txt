Subtopic: 5.1.3 What is Signal Filtering?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
So what exactly is signal filtering? Formally, it's the process of selectively removing unwanted frequency components from a recorded signal, while preserving the information that's relevant for analysis. An important point: filtering does not create new information. It improves the quality of the recorded signal by reducing components that are unlikely to contain useful physiological information. Consider an EEG recording. The recording contains the electrical activity generated by the brain, which is the signal we're interested in. But the same recording may also contain slow baseline drift, electrical interference from nearby equipment, muscle activity from the face or neck, and power-line interference. If all of these components remain in the recording, identifying meaningful brain activity becomes much more difficult. Filtering helps separate the signal of interest from these unwanted components. And filtering is a selective process. A properly designed filter removes only those components that are considered undesirable, while preserving the physiological information we need for analysis. The key definition: signal filtering is the process of preserving useful signal components while attenuating unwanted components.
