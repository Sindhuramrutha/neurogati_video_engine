Subtopic: Introduction to Spikes and Local Field Potentials
Scene type: manim

Narration:
So far in this course, we've been looking at neural signals recorded at relatively large spatial scales, like EEG, which gives us a useful view of coordinated activity across large populations of neurons. But sometimes we need to get much closer to the source. By placing electrodes directly inside or near neural tissue, we can record electrical signals at a much smaller spatial scale. Two important signals come out of these recordings: neural spikes and local field potentials, or LFPs. These two signals give us complementary information. A spike tells us about the rapid electrical activity associated with a neuron firing, while an LFP reflects slower electrical activity generated by populations of neurons near the recording electrode. Understanding both is important because it lets us move from studying large-scale brain activity toward studying individual neurons, local populations, and neural circuits.
