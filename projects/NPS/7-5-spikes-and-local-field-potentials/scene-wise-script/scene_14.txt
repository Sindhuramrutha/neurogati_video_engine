Subtopic: Summary and Section Recap
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Let's recap what we've learned. Spikes and local field potentials provide two complementary views of neural activity. Neural spikes are brief, high-frequency electrical events associated with neuronal firing. Because they are small and short in duration, they require appropriate filtering and detection methods. Spike sorting can help separate activity from different neurons. Local field potentials represent slower electrical activity arising from populations of neurons near the recording electrode. They provide information about local population activity and neural oscillations. Together, these signals can be used to investigate neural coding, how information is represented in neural activity; circuit analysis, how different neural populations interact; and neural prostheses, how neural activity can be decoded to control external devices. We've now moved from signals generated by individual neurons and local populations toward another non-invasive recording modality that measures neural activity from outside the head. In the next section, we'll examine Magnetoencephalography, or MEG, and see how magnetic fields generated by neural activity can be used for functional brain mapping and studying the timing of neural processes.
