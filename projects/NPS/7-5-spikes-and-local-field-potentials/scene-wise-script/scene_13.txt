Subtopic: Signal-Processing Perspective
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Spikes and LFPs also demonstrate why signal processing is essential in neuroscience. A raw extracellular recording contains a mixture of different components. We can conceptually separate it into: raw extracellular signal, then filtering, then a high-frequency spike component and a lower-frequency LFP component, then feature extraction, and finally neural interpretation or decoding. For spikes, processing may involve high-pass filtering, spike detection, and spike sorting. For LFPs, analysis can involve low-frequency filtering, spectral analysis, and time-frequency analysis. The important point is that the recording itself is not yet the final information. Signal processing helps us transform the raw electrical measurement into meaningful neural features.
