Subtopic: Neural Spikes: Definition
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Let's start with neural spikes. A neural spike, also called an extracellular action-potential waveform or unit spike, is a brief electrical signal associated with the firing of a neuron. When a neuron receives enough excitatory input, its membrane potential can reach a firing threshold and generate an action potential. An action potential can be measured in different ways. In an intracellular recording, the electrode is positioned inside the neuron and measures the potential difference across the neuronal membrane. In an extracellular recording, the electrode is positioned outside the neuron and detects the electrical field generated by nearby neuronal activity. The resulting extracellular waveform is what we usually call a spike.

Animation description:
Show a single neuron with a cell body, dendrites, and an axon. Illustrate an intracellular electrode penetrating the neuron, with a trace showing a large action potential, around 100 millivolts, with a clear depolarization and repolarization. Then show a separate extracellular electrode positioned just outside the neuron. Animate the neuron firing an action potential, with a traveling wave along the axon, and show the much smaller extracellular waveform being detected. Label the extracellular waveform 'spike'. Use a side-by-side comparison of the intracellular and extracellular signals to emphasize the difference in amplitude.
