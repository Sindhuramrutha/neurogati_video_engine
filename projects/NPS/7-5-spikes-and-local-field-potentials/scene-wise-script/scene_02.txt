Subtopic: Neural Spikes: Waveform, Amplitude, and Duration
Scene type: manim

Narration:
The shape of an extracellular spike depends on several factors: the type of neuron, the location of the recording electrode, the distance between the neuron and the electrode, the geometry of the neuron, and the properties of the electrode. For example, spikes recorded near the soma can have a dominant negative peak, whereas recordings near dendrites can show different waveform characteristics. So the waveform itself contains useful information about the underlying neuronal activity. Extracellular spikes are very small signals. While an intracellular action potential can exceed about 100 millivolts, extracellular spikes are generally much smaller, often around 100 microvolts or below. Spike amplitude also decreases rapidly as the distance between the neuron and electrode increases. Spikes are also very brief. A typical extracellular spike may last only around 2 to 3 milliseconds and contains substantial high-frequency content. Much of this spike-related activity lies roughly in the 300 hertz to several kilohertz range. This is one reason signal processing is essential when working with spikes.
