Subtopic: How Does a High-Pass Filter Affect a Signal?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Now think about an EEG recording with slow baseline drift caused by gradual changes in electrode contact. The neural activity itself might be there, but the drifting baseline makes the whole waveform hard to interpret. When a high-pass filter is applied, several things happen: the slow baseline drift is reduced, the baseline becomes more stable, and the faster neural activity becomes easier to observe. So the filter improves the visibility of the physiological signal by reducing unwanted low-frequency components. But here is the crucial caveat: the filter cannot distinguish between useful and unwanted low-frequency information. Any physiological activity occurring below the selected cutoff frequency will also be attenuated. That is why the cutoff frequency must always be selected carefully.
