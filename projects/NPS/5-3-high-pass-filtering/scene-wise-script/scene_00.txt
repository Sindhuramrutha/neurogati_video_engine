Narration:
In the previous section, we looked at low-pass filters — tools that keep low-frequency components and reduce high-frequency ones. But not every unwanted signal lives at high frequencies. Many neural recordings contain slow fluctuations that have nothing to do with the physiological signal we actually care about. These slow variations can come from electrode movement, from respiration, from changes in electrode impedance, or from gradual shifts in the recording baseline itself. And these low-frequency components can obscure important neural activity, making later analysis much harder. To reduce these unwanted slow components while preserving the higher-frequency information, we turn to a different tool: the high-pass filter. In this section, we'll examine how a high-pass filter works, understand what the cutoff frequency means in high-pass filtering, and discuss its applications in neural signal processing.
