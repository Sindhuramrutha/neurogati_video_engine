Subtopic: Limitations
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Although ERP analysis is a powerful technique, it also has several limitations. The most important limitation is that the event-related response is often much smaller than the ongoing EEG activity. For this reason, many repeated trials are usually required to obtain a reliable ERP waveform. In addition, ERP analysis assumes that the neural response occurs at approximately the same time following each presentation of the stimulus. If the timing of the response varies substantially between trials, averaging may reduce the visibility of the ERP. So an important note: an ERP is not obtained from a single EEG recording. It is typically obtained by averaging many EEG epochs that are aligned to the same experimental event. This averaging process is the key reason why ERPs can reveal neural responses that are difficult to observe in individual trials.

On the right, multiple epochs where the response latency varies from trial to trial, and the averaged result shows a smeared or reduced peak.
