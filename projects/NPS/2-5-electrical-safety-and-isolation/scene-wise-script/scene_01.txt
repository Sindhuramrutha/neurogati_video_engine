Subtopic: Why is Electrical Safety Important?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Electrical safety means taking measures to protect participants, operators, and equipment from electrical hazards during biomedical signal recording. Here's the key difference between these systems and ordinary electronic measurements: neural signal acquisition involves direct contact between electronic equipment and the human body. EEG electrodes sit on the scalp. EMG electrodes go on the skin or are inserted into muscles. ECoG electrodes are placed directly on the surface of the brain during surgery. These systems work with extremely small biological signals, but they're still connected to powered electronic devices. So any unintended current reaching the participant could pose a serious safety risk. This is why electrical safety isn't just good laboratory practice — it's a fundamental requirement of every biomedical recording system.
