Subtopic: 7.1.2 Brain-State Analysis - Applications and Advantages
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Studying brain states using EEG has numerous applications in neuroscience and clinical practice. Some important examples include: cognitive neuroscience, where researchers investigate how attention, memory, language, and decision-making influence neural activity by comparing EEG recordings across different cognitive tasks; mental workload assessment, where changes in EEG activity can provide information about mental effort, fatigue, and sustained attention during complex tasks; consciousness research, where EEG is widely used to study transitions between wakefulness, sleep, anaesthesia, and altered states of consciousness; and neurofeedback, where real-time EEG measurements can be used to provide feedback to individuals, allowing them to learn to voluntarily regulate aspects of their own brain activity. EEG offers several advantages for studying brain states: excellent temporal resolution, non-invasive recording, continuous monitoring of brain activity, suitable for long-duration recordings, and compatible with real-time signal processing. These characteristics make EEG one of the most widely used tools for monitoring dynamic changes in brain function. Brain states are not identified from a single EEG sample. Instead, they are determined by analysing patterns of neural activity over time using signal processing techniques such as spectral analysis, spectrograms, and time-frequency analysis.
