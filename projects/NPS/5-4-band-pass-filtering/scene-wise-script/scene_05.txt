Subtopic: 5.4.6 Applications in Neural Signal Processing
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Band‑pass filtering is one of the most widely used preprocessing techniques in neuroscience. In EEG, recordings are commonly band‑pass filtered to retain the frequency range relevant to the analysis while suppressing slow drift and high‑frequency interference. For EMG, signals occupy a characteristic frequency range, and band‑pass filtering helps suppress low‑frequency motion artefacts and high‑frequency electrical noise, preserving muscle activity. Local field potentials are often band‑pass filtered to isolate specific oscillatory frequency bands. And in brain–computer interfaces, band‑pass filtering is an essential preprocessing step before feature extraction and classification.
