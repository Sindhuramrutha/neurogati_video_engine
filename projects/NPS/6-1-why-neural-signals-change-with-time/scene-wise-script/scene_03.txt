Subtopic: 6.1.4 What is a Nonstationary Signal?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Now consider the opposite case. A nonstationary signal is one whose statistical or spectral characteristics change over time. Think of an EEG recording obtained while a participant performs a cognitive task. At the beginning of the recording, the participant may be relaxed with their eyes closed. A few seconds later, they open their eyes. Soon afterward, they respond to a visual stimulus. Later, they perform a memory task. Throughout this recording, neural activity changes continuously. Some oscillations become stronger, others become weaker. New frequency components may appear, while existing ones disappear. The signal recorded at the beginning is therefore different from the signal recorded later. This type of signal is called nonstationary, because its characteristics evolve with time. Unlike stationary signals, nonstationary signals cannot be fully described using a single frequency spectrum for the entire recording.
