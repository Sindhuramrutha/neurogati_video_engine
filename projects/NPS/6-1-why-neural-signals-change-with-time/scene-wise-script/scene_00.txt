Subtopic: 6.1.1 Introduction
Scene type: manim

Narration:
In the previous module, we saw how filtering improves the quality of neural recordings by removing unwanted signal components. We also learned that neural signals can be analysed in the frequency domain, to identify the oscillations present within a recording. At this point, an important question naturally arises. Do the frequencies present in a neural signal remain constant throughout the entire recording? For many real-world signals, including neural recordings, the answer is no. Neural activity is continuously changing. A person may transition from rest to movement, respond to an external stimulus, fall asleep, or perform a cognitive task. Each of these events changes the underlying neural activity, and consequently the characteristics of the recorded signal also change over time. Understanding these changes is the first step toward analysing nonstationary neural signals.
