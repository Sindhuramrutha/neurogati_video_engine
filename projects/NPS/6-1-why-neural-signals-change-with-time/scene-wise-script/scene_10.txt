Subtopic: 6.1.7 The Shift in Question — and 6.1.8 Summary
Scene type: manim

Narration:
So the question in neural signal analysis is no longer simply: which frequencies are present? Instead, we ask: which frequencies are present, and when do they occur? This question motivates the development of time–frequency analysis, which will be introduced in the next section. To summarise what we've learned in this section: we introduced the concept of nonstationary neural signals. We learned that neural recordings are generally nonstationary, because brain activity changes continuously in response to behaviour, sensory stimulation, cognitive processes, and physiological state. Unlike stationary signals, nonstationary signals cannot be fully described using a single frequency spectrum, because their frequency content changes over time. Understanding this limitation prepares us for the next topic: the Short-Time Fourier Transform, or STFT, which extends conventional frequency analysis by incorporating information about both time and frequency.
