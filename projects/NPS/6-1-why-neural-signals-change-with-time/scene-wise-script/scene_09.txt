Subtopic: 6.1.7 Why Does This Create a Problem?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Now, why does this create a problem? At first glance, analysing the frequency content of a neural signal seems straightforward. However, most classical frequency analysis methods assume that the signal remains unchanged throughout the observation period. If the frequency content changes during the recording, then calculating a single frequency spectrum for the entire signal provides only an overall average. It does not indicate when specific frequencies were present. For example, suppose alpha activity is present only during the first ten seconds of an EEG recording. A conventional Fourier Transform may indicate that alpha frequencies exist somewhere in the recording, but it cannot identify the exact time at which they occurred. For many neuroscience applications, this information is essential. We often need to know not only what frequencies are present, but also when they occur.
