Subtopic: 6.2.1 Introduction
Scene type: manim

Narration:
In the previous section, we learned that neural signals are generally nonstationary. Their frequency content changes continuously as neural activity evolves over time. We also identified an important limitation of the conventional Fourier Transform. Although it tells us which frequencies are present in a signal, it cannot tell us when those frequencies occur. And for many neuroscience applications, that timing information is absolutely essential. Imagine analysing an EEG recording during a visual experiment. Suppose alpha activity appears only after the participant closes their eyes. The Fourier Transform can indicate that alpha frequencies exist somewhere in the recording, but it cannot identify the exact moment at which they appeared. To solve this problem, we need a method that analyses both time and frequency simultaneously. One of the earliest and most widely used solutions is the Short-Time Fourier Transform, commonly abbreviated as STFT.
