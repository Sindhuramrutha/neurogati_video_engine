Subtopic: 6.2.3 Why Do We Need STFT?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Let's begin by revisiting the problem. Suppose you record an EEG signal for thirty seconds. During the first ten seconds, alpha activity is dominant. During the next ten seconds, beta activity becomes stronger. Finally, during the last ten seconds, gamma activity appears briefly. If we apply a conventional Fourier Transform to the entire thirty-second recording, the result is a single frequency spectrum representing the entire signal. Although this spectrum tells us that alpha, beta, and gamma frequencies are present, it provides no information about when each frequency occurred. This is a significant limitation, because neural activity is dynamic. In neuroscience, timing is often just as important as frequency. To understand brain function, we need a method that tells us both: which frequencies are present, and at what time do they occur? This is precisely the problem that the Short-Time Fourier Transform was designed to solve.
