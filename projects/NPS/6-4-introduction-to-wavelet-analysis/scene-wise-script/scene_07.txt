Subtopic: Summary and Transition to ERP
Scene type: manim

Narration:
In this section, we introduced wavelet analysis, an adaptive time–frequency analysis technique designed to overcome the fixed-window limitation of the Short-Time Fourier Transform. We learned that wavelet analysis examines signals at multiple scales, providing good time resolution for rapid events and good frequency resolution for slower oscillations. This adaptability makes wavelet analysis particularly useful for analysing highly nonstationary neural signals, such as EEG recordings containing transient neural events. So far, we have focused on methods for analysing how neural activity changes over time and across different frequencies. However, many neuroscience experiments are designed around specific events, such as the presentation of a visual image, an auditory tone, or a button press. Rather than analysing continuous neural activity, researchers often wish to measure the brain's response to these events. One of the most widely used approaches for this purpose is the Event-Related Potential, or ERP, which we will explore in the next section.
