Subtopic: 2.3.4 What is an Amplifier?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
An amplifier is an electronic circuit designed to increase the strength of a weak electrical signal. It is important to understand that an amplifier does not create new information. Instead, it increases the magnitude of the existing signal so that it can be processed more accurately by the remaining components of the acquisition system. For example, if an EEG signal has an amplitude of only a few microvolts, the amplifier increases this voltage to a level that electronic circuits can process reliably, while preserving the original waveform. A good amplifier should increase the desired signal while introducing as little additional noise or distortion as possible. One important note: the purpose of amplification is not to improve the quality of the signal. Its primary purpose is to increase the signal level so that subsequent processing stages can operate correctly.
