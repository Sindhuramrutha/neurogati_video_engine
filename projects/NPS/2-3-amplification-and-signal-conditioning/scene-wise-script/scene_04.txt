Subtopic: 2.3.5 Signal Conditioning
Scene type: manim

Narration:
After amplification, the recorded signal still cannot be sent directly to the computer. The signal may contain unwanted electrical interference, baseline drift, or other components that reduce recording quality. Before digitisation, the signal is therefore prepared through a process known as signal conditioning. Signal conditioning refers to the collection of operations that prepare the biological signal for accurate digital conversion. Depending on the recording system, signal conditioning may include: initial filtering, noise reduction, baseline stabilisation, impedance matching, and signal isolation. The exact conditioning process depends on the recording modality and the design of the acquisition system. And here's the key definition: signal conditioning refers to all operations performed on a biological signal to prepare it for accurate digitisation and analysis.
