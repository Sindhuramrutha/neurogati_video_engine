Subtopic: 2.3.3 Why Do Neural Signals Need Amplification?
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Imagine trying to hear someone whispering in a crowded room. Even though the person is speaking, their voice is so weak that it is easily overwhelmed by surrounding noise. Neural signals present a similar challenge. The electrical activity produced by neurons or muscles is very small. These signals must travel through biological tissue, electrodes, connecting cables, and electronic circuits before reaching the recording system. During this journey, they are easily affected by environmental interference and electronic noise. If these signals are not strengthened before further processing, important physiological information may be lost. Therefore, the first electronic component after the electrode is the amplifier. And here's the key definition: amplification is the process of increasing the amplitude of a biological signal without altering the information it contains.
