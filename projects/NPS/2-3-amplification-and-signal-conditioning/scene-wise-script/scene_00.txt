Subtopic: 2.3.1 Introduction
Scene type: manim

Narration:
In the previous section, we saw how electrodes and sensors detect biological signals. But detecting a signal is only the first step. The electrical signals generated by the human body are extremely small. For example, EEG signals are typically measured in microvolts — that's millions of times smaller than the voltages used by most electronic devices. If these tiny signals were sent directly to a computer, they would be difficult to distinguish from electrical noise and could not be processed reliably. To overcome this problem, every neural recording system includes a stage known as amplification and signal conditioning. In this section, we'll understand why amplification is necessary, how amplifiers improve signal quality, and what is meant by signal conditioning.
