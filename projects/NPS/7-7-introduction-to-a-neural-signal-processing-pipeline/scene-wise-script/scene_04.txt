Subtopic: 7.7.5 Stage 4: Interpretation or Classification
Scene type: manim

Narration:
After extracting relevant features, we need to determine what those features mean. There are two broad possibilities. The first is interpretation. In some studies, researchers use the extracted features to understand neural activity. For example, EEG leads to frequency analysis, which shows increased alpha power, leading to an interpretation of brain activity. The goal is not necessarily to assign the signal to a predefined class, but to understand what is happening in the nervous system. The second possibility is classification. In other applications, the extracted features are used to assign the signal to a particular category. For example, EEG goes through preprocessing, feature extraction, and then a classifier to determine sleep stage. A machine-learning model might classify a segment of EEG as wake, NREM sleep, or REM sleep. Similarly, a brain-computer interface may classify neural activity into different intended commands.
