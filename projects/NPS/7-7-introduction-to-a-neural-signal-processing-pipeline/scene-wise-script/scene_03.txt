Subtopic: 7.7.4 Stage 3: Feature Extraction
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Once the signal has been cleaned, the next question is: what information should we extract from it? Feature extraction is the process of converting a neural signal into quantitative measurements that capture relevant characteristics of the signal. The appropriate features depend on the application. For an EEG signal, possible features include signal amplitude, frequency-band power, spectral characteristics, time-frequency features, and event-related responses. For spike recordings, features may include spike count, firing rate, spike timing, and spike waveform characteristics. For EMG, features could describe the strength or temporal characteristics of muscle activity. The important principle is: raw waveform becomes relevant measurable features. Feature extraction reduces a complex neural recording into information that can be analyzed more easily.
