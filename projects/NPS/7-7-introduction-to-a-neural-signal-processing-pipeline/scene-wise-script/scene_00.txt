Subtopic: 7.7.1 Introduction to Neural Signal Processing
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Throughout this course, we've studied individual stages of neural signal processing. We learned how neural signals are generated and recorded, how noise and artifacts affect recordings, how filtering can improve signal quality, and how time- and frequency-domain methods can reveal useful information from neural signals. We've also seen that different recording modalities — like EEG, ECoG, EMG, EOG, spikes, LFPs, and MEG — are used for different neuroscience applications. But in a real neuroscience experiment, these methods are not used independently. They're combined into a signal-processing pipeline that takes us from a raw recording to a meaningful scientific or clinical result. The basic idea is: raw signal, then preprocessing, then feature extraction, then interpretation or classification. In this final section, we'll bring together the major concepts from the course and see how they fit into one complete workflow.
