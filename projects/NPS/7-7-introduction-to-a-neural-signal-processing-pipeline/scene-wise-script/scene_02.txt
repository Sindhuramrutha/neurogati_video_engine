Subtopic: 7.7.3 Stage 2: Preprocessing
Scene type: manim

Narration:
Stage two is preprocessing. Preprocessing refers to the operations used to prepare the raw neural signal for further analysis. The goal is not simply to make the signal look cleaner. The goal is to reduce unwanted components while preserving the neural information relevant to the research question. Preprocessing may include filtering, artifact detection, artifact removal, signal segmentation, baseline correction, and normalization. For example, an EEG recording may contain power-line interference, eye-blink artifacts, muscle activity, and baseline drift. Appropriate preprocessing can reduce these unwanted components. This connects directly to the earlier modules of the course, where we studied filtering, artifacts, and signal cleaning.
