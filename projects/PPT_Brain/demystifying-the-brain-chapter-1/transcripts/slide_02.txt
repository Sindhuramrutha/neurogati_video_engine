So here's Lewis Carroll, the author of Alice in Wonderland, writing in 1890, in a novel called Sylvie and Bruno, and he's imagining the hand moving because certain forces from the brain are impressed upon it — a kind of nerve-force stored in the brain, which he speculates could be traced back to chemical forces supplied by the blood. And that really sets up the theme for this lecture: the history of neuroscience is a long journey filled with errors and breakthroughs, moving from ancient misconceptions — like the idea that the heart was the seat of consciousness — all the way to modern gene therapies, where we're now mapping the complex electrical networks of the mind.
