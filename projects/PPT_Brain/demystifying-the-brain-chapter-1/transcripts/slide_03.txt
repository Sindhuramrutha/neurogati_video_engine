So before modern science, where intelligence actually lives in the body was a genuinely fierce philosophical fight. On one side you had Hippocrates, the father of Western medicine, who already in the fourth and third centuries B.C. correctly pointed to the brain as the seat of sensation and intelligence, and Plato backed him up, because tying the physical brain to his philosophical ideals just fit his worldview. But then you have Aristotle, who for all his brilliance got this one badly wrong: he argued the heart was the seat of consciousness, and probably the thing that misled him was the observation that the body dies when the heart stops, yet it can survive a brain injury.
