So here we have Galen, who's often called the restorer of anatomy. Because religious restrictions prevented him from dissecting human bodies, this Greek physician turned to animal cadavers to study the brain — and while he became a true master of anatomy, his ideas about function remained flawed for centuries afterward. On the anatomical side, he correctly identified the ventricles, the pia mater, and the hemispheres, and he even distinguished between autonomous nerves serving the internal organs and somatic nerves controlling voice and movement. But when it came to function, he had no microscopic tools, so he believed that "animal spirits," or pneumata, surged through the nerves to produce movement and settled into the ventricles when the body was at rest.
