For nearly fifteen hundred years, the split between studying structure and studying function held. Leonardo da Vinci, the artist-scientist, compared dissecting the brain to peeling an onion, and although his anatomical drawings were remarkable, he still followed the ancient tradition of attributing cognitive functions to the ventricles. René Descartes, by contrast, proposed a radical answer to the mind–body problem in the form of dualism, arguing that the body operates as a machine governed by physical laws while the mind is a non-material entity—a position that let scientists set the soul aside and investigate the brain as a biological machine.
