# Assets & Design

Extraction pulls every picture out of the source documents and knows nothing
about which ones matter. A single deck yields sixty pictures and half of them
are bullets, logos and divider rules. Your job is to say which ones a viewer
would be glad to see, and to carry the source material's own look forward.

## What to decide

**Use, or skip.** A picture is worth using when it carries information the words
cannot: a diagram, a chart, a photograph of apparatus, a labelled screenshot.
Skip decoration — bullets, rules, logos, background flourishes, and any picture
whose content is already fully stated in the narration.

**Say why in one line.** "A logo" and "the only diagram of the acquisition chain"
are both useful notes. A skipped picture with no reason invites someone to
re-decide it from scratch later.

## Rules

**A picture is evidence.** It came from a specific place in a specific document,
and it keeps that place. Never present a picture from one section as if it
illustrated another.

**A drawn picture is not the author's.** Anything generated is marked as
generated. If a drawn picture is used, the video is showing something the source
did not contain, and that has to stay visible to the person reviewing it.

**Small is not automatically worthless, but it usually is.** Report the
measurement rather than guessing: a 40x40 picture at the top of every slide is a
logo, and an 800x600 one under a heading is a figure.

**Do not invent a look.** The colours and fonts are the source deck's, read from
its theme. If none was extracted, say so; do not choose a palette.

## What you are given

The extracted content, the figure inventory with each picture's real dimensions
and where it came from, and any decisions already made. Decisions already made
are respected — say when you disagree, and why, rather than reversing them.
