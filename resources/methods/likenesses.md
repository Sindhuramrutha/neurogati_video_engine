# How real people looked

From their best-known portraits. The art director reads this before describing a
real person: the planning model drew Golgi with a full beard in three videos running,
and Lewis Carroll as a white-bearded old man. For someone not listed, describe only
what is well documented -- or show them from behind, at work, or by their apparatus.

- **Lewis Carroll** (Charles Dodgson): clean-shaven, dark wavy hair to the collar, a
  slim face.
- **Camillo Golgi**: a large, thick drooping moustache and no beard; balding in later
  life.
- **Santiago Ramón y Cajal**: a full beard and moustache (dark in middle age, grey to
  white later), a high balding forehead.
- **Ivan Pavlov**: a full beard and moustache, white in his later years; balding.
- **René Descartes**: dark hair to the shoulders, a thin moustache and a small tuft of
  beard under the lip.
- **Leonardo da Vinci**: long hair and a long flowing beard, as in the red-chalk
  portrait of his old age.
- **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek**: a long curled wig and a small moustache.
- **Luigi Galvani**: clean-shaven, an eighteenth-century powdered wig.
- **Alessandro Volta**: clean-shaven, wavy hair.
- **Franz Joseph Gall**: clean-shaven, a broad high forehead.
- **Otto Loewi**: a moustache, balding.
- **B. F. Skinner**: clean-shaven, receding hair, glasses in his later years.
- **Robert Hooke**: no portrait from life survives -- never a face; show him at his
  microscope from behind, or his drawings.
- **Hippocrates, Aristotle, Galen**: no likeness from life -- bearded, as on the
  classical busts, never a specific invented face.
